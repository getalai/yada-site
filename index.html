What it does, and doesn't

Any key. Really any key.

Right ⌘ by default, because nobody uses it. Prefer Fn, Caps Lock, a function key, or ⌃⌥Space? Press it once in Settings and that's your key. Double-tap it for hands-free when you have a lot to say.

Works in every app

There is nothing to connect or set up per app. Put your cursor where the words should go and hold the key.

Handles how people actually talk

Punctuation and capitals are taken care of. Speak in whichever language you think in, or mix two in one sentence. Turn on cleanup and it also drops the ums and applies your "no wait, Wednesday" corrections.

Keeps nothing

Audio streams to the speech service only while you hold the key, and is not stored anywhere. Your transcripts stay on your Mac, where you can delete them whenever you like. No account, no sign-in.

Why another one

Dictation should be as free as typing. Somewhere along the way it became a twelve-to-fifteen-dollar subscription, with Wispr Flow at the front of the line.

Yada is the part everyone actually uses: hold a key, talk, let go. No accounts, no dashboard, no plans. The speech recognition behind it costs a few cents an hour, and the app is written to stay out of the way. That keeps it cheap to run, and cheap to run means free to use.

Free and meant to stay that way
If enough people use it that the speech bills add up, it may ask for a dollar or two a month to cover them. That's the whole plan.

How it works

  1. Click where you want the text. Any app.
  2. Hold your key (right ⌘ out of the box) and say it. A small pill shows the words as they come.
  3. Let go. The text is pasted in. Press esc while holding to throw it away.

Yada is in a private beta. The first time you open it, it asks for an invite code. If you don't have one, ask for one.

Questions

Which Macs?
Apple silicon (M1 and later) on macOS 13 Ventura or newer. Intel isn't supported yet.
What permissions does it need?
Microphone, to hear you. Accessibility, to type into other apps and to notice the key. Input Monitoring on some macOS versions. It asks for each one when it's needed and says why.
Does it type into password fields?
No. When macOS has secure input on, Yada steps aside and tells you.
What does the speech recognition run on?
Meta's Muse Voice Transcribe, through a small relay, with the zero-retention flag set on every session. Nothing you say is stored by Meta or by us.
Is it free?
Yes, and it's meant to stay free. The only cost is the speech recognition, a few cents an hour. If usage ever grows to where that adds up, Yada may ask for a dollar or two a month to cover it. Nothing about the app changes either way.
